Welcome to my personal website. This is the first post on what I hope will become a record of my journey through research, engineering, and life in Tokyo.

## The Beginning: Language and Chemistry

My path to where I am today wasn't a straight line. It started in 2016 when I enrolled at Hallym Japanese Language Institute, determined to build a life and career in Japan. After a year of intensive language study, I moved into Tokai University's Japanese Language Research Department, and from there into the Faculty of Engineering, Department of Applied Chemistry.

Those four years at Tokai University were formative. I built a solid foundation in engineering and energy systems — learning to think like an engineer, to break down complex problems into manageable components, and to approach challenges with a systems mindset. This foundation later led to my work on [digital twin modeling of syngas-to-methanol conversion](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.7776863), for which I received the Best Presentation Award at the International Scientific-Practical Conference on Sustainable Development Goals in 2023.

## The Pivot: From Engineering to Social Science

After graduating from Tokai in 2022, I took a step that surprised even myself: I enrolled as a research student at Sophia University's Graduate School of Global Studies. This was my first real exposure to the social sciences, and it opened my eyes to how much of the world's most pressing problems require not just technical solutions, but policy and economic understanding.

This led me to Waseda University, where I was accepted as a MEXT (Japanese Government Scholarship) research student in the Graduate School of International Communication. The MEXT scholarship was a turning point — it gave me the freedom and support to pursue research full-time.

## Waseda: Where It All Comes Together

In April 2024, I enrolled in Waseda University's Graduate School of Social Sciences, specializing in Policy Science. Here, I finally found the intersection I'd been looking for — a place where my engineering background meets econometric analysis meets real-world policy questions.

My current work focuses on mathematical modeling for policy prediction using large-scale data with Python and Stata. The goal: to use data not just to describe the world, but to forecast how policy decisions will shape it.

## Parallel Track: Apple Japan

Alongside my academic work, I joined Apple Japan in June 2024 as a Technical Expert. In this role, I lead team performance analysis and mentoring in a multilingual, multicultural environment. It's a completely different kind of challenge from academic research — but the core skill is the same: identify the problem from the data, and build a process that maximizes outcomes.

## What's Next

I'm currently working toward CFA Level I certification, which will add financial analysis to my toolkit. My goal is to bring together the "engineering, economics, and practice" triad at a higher level — using quantitative analysis and global leadership to solve complex business and policy challenges.
