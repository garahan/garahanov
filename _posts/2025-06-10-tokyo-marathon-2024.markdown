On a cold February morning in 2024, I stood at the starting line of the Tokyo Marathon with 38,000 other runners. I had trained for months, but nothing prepares you for the moment when the crowd surges forward and 42.195 kilometers stretches out ahead of you.

## Why I Decided to Run

I'm not a natural athlete. I played football (soccer) in university — I was the captain of the student international team — but distance running was never my thing. The idea of running for 4+ hours seemed absurd.

But I needed a challenge that was entirely outside my academic and professional life. Something physical, measurable, and with a clear finish line. The Tokyo Marathon lottery gave me a spot, and that was that.

## The Training

Training for a marathon is an exercise in consistency. No single run matters much; what matters is showing up every week, rain or shine, for months.

| Phase | Weekly Distance | Key Sessions |
|-------|----------------|-------------|
| Base (Weeks 1–8) | 30–40 km | Easy runs, building aerobic base |
| Build (Weeks 9–16) | 40–55 km | Long runs, tempo runs, intervals |
| Peak (Weeks 17–20) | 55–65 km | Longest runs (30+ km) |
| Taper (Weeks 21–24) | 20–30 km | Reduce volume, maintain intensity |

The hardest part wasn't the physical effort — it was the time management. Fitting 5 runs per week around a master's program and a job at Apple required planning every day down to the hour.

## Race Day

The first 20 km felt easy. The crowd support in Tokyo is incredible — people line the entire route, handing out food, shouting encouragement, playing music.

At 30 km, I hit the wall. My legs felt like lead. Every step was a negotiation with myself: *just keep moving*. This is where marathon running becomes mental, not physical.

The last 5 km were the longest of my life. But crossing the finish line — that moment is indescribable. Not because of the medal, but because I proved to myself that I can commit to something for months and see it through.

## What It Taught Me

1. **Consistency beats intensity** — One great run doesn't matter; 100 good runs do. The same is true for research and skill-building.
2. **The wall is real, and it's mental** — At 30 km, my body wanted to stop. My mind kept it going. In research, the "wall" is when experiments fail or results are ambiguous. Pushing through is the skill.
3. **Preparation is everything** — Race day was the easy part. The 4 months of training were the real marathon. In academia, the paper is the race; the months of data analysis are the training.
4. **Community matters** — Running with others, sharing tips, encouraging each other — it made the process bearable. The same is true for research: find your community.

## What's Next

I'm already thinking about my next challenge. Maybe a sub-4-hour marathon. Maybe a triathlon. The specific goal matters less than having one.

If you're considering running a marathon — or any big goal that seems impossible — my advice is simple: start small, be consistent, and trust the process. The finish line comes faster than you think.
