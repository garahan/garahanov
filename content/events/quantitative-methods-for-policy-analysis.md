My master’s thesis examined whether export-market diversification is associated
with lower economic volatility in natural-gas-exporting countries.

## Research design

I constructed a balanced panel of 15 countries covering 2003–2023. Economic
volatility was measured using the rolling standard deviation of real GDP per
capita growth. Export-market diversification was derived from an inverse
Herfindahl–Hirschman Index, and institutional quality was represented by the
World Bank’s Government Effectiveness indicator.

The dataset combined UN Comtrade trade data, World Bank development and
governance indicators, international gas-price data, and national energy
statistics.

## Estimation

The analysis used country fixed-effects regressions, interaction models,
diagnostic tests, and Driscoll–Kraay robust standard errors. These methods were
selected to account for unobserved country characteristics and common forms of
dependence in macroeconomic panel data.

## Main finding

Greater export-market diversification was associated with lower economic
volatility. The relationship was strongest where institutional capacity was
weaker, supporting a substitution interpretation between diversification and
institutional strength.
