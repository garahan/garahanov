Welcome to my personal website. This is the first post on what I hope will become a record of my journey through research, engineering, and life in Tokyo.

## The Beginning: Language and Chemistry

My path to where I am today wasn't a straight line. It started in 2016 when I enrolled at Hallym Japanese Language Institute, determined to build a life and career in Japan. After a year of intensive language study, I moved into Tokai University's Japanese Language Research Department, and from there into the Faculty of Engineering, Department of Applied Chemistry.

Those four years at Tokai University were formative. I built a solid foundation in engineering and energy systems — learning to think like an engineer, to break down complex problems into manageable components, and to approach challenges with a systems mindset. This foundation later led to my work on [digital twin modeling of syngas-to-methanol conversion](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.7776863), for which I received the Best Presentation Award at the International Scientific-Practical Conference on Sustainable Development Goals in 2023.

## The Pivot: From Molecules to Models

After graduating with a BEng in Applied Chemistry in 2022, I made a decision that surprised many of my peers: I shifted toward the social sciences. I spent a semester as a research student at Sophia University's Graduate School of Global Studies, then received a MEXT Scholarship to join Waseda University.

At Waseda, I enrolled in the Graduate School of Social Sciences, specializing in Policy Science. The transition was natural for me — I had always been interested in how systems work, and policy is just another kind of system. The same analytical thinking that I used to model chemical reactions could be applied to model social and economic outcomes.

## Where I Am Now

Today, I'm simultaneously pursuing my MA at Waseda and working as a Technical Expert at Apple Japan. My research uses Python and Stata for econometric modeling and policy prediction. At Apple, I lead team performance analysis and mentoring in a multilingual environment.

The thread connecting all of this is data-driven decision-making. Whether I'm modeling chemical processes, predicting policy outcomes, or analyzing team performance, the approach is the same: observe, measure, model, decide.

This website is where I'll document that journey.
