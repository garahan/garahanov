On a cold February morning in 2024, I stood at the starting line of the Tokyo Marathon with 38,000 other runners. I had trained for months, but nothing prepares you for the moment when the crowd surges forward and 42.195 kilometers stretches out ahead of you.

## Why I Decided to Run

I'm not a natural athlete. I played football (soccer) in university — I was the captain of the student international team — but distance running was never my thing. The idea of running for 4+ hours seemed absurd.

But I needed a challenge that was entirely outside my academic and professional life. Something physical, measurable, and with a clear finish line. The Tokyo Marathon lottery gave me a spot, and that was that.

## The Training

I followed a 16-week training plan, building from 5km runs to a 32km long run three weeks before the race. The training taught me something unexpected: the value of boring, repetitive consistency. There's no shortcut in marathon training. You just have to put in the miles, week after week, rain or shine.

This maps directly to research. There's no flash of genius that replaces the daily work of data cleaning, model testing, and writing. The marathon reminded me that big goals are achieved through small, unglamorous steps.

## Race Day

The first 30km felt manageable. Then the wall hit. My legs felt like lead, and every step required a conscious decision to continue. This is where the mental training matters more than the physical.

I finished in 4 hours and 12 minutes. Not fast, but I finished. And crossing that finish line — that's a feeling I'll carry for the rest of my life.

## What I Learned

1. **Big goals are achieved through small steps.** 42km is overwhelming. 1km, repeated 42 times, is manageable.
2. **Consistency beats intensity.** The 16 weeks of training mattered more than any single run.
3. **The wall is real, but it's mental.** Your body can do more than your mind believes.
4. **Finish lines matter.** Having a clear endpoint transforms the journey.

I'm already thinking about my next marathon. Once you know you can finish, the question becomes: how fast?
